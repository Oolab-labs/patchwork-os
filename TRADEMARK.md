# Trademark policy

The code is MIT. The name is not.

This is the standard open-core arrangement, and the reason for it is narrow: the
MIT licence lets anyone copy, modify and redistribute the software, which is
intended. It does not follow that a modified copy should be able to call itself
Patchwork OS. A user who downloads something called "Patchwork OS" should get
this project, not a fork with different behaviour — otherwise the name stops
carrying information, which harms users rather than competitors.

Nothing in this policy restricts what you may do with the **code**. See
[LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md) for that.

## 2. Uses that need no permission

You do not need to ask before doing any of the following. These are ordinary,
truthful references to what this project is — nominative use.

- **Stating compatibility or that you build on it.** "Built on Patchwork OS",
  "works with Patchwork OS", "a Patchwork OS plugin", "compatible with Patchwork
  OS recipes". Say it in prose, in your README, in documentation, in a
  conference talk.
- **Naming it accurately in comparison or commentary.** Reviews, benchmarks,
  tutorials, criticism, academic work. You do not need our approval to say
  something unflattering, and we will not use trademark law to answer criticism.
- **Referring to the software you are running.** Blog posts, deployment guides,
  job adverts, support documentation.
- **Package and repository names that describe a relationship**, where the
  relationship is real and the naming does not read as an official release —
  e.g. `patchwork-os-plugin-jira`, `my-org/patchwork-recipes`. Prefer the
  documented plugin prefix conventions where they exist.
- **Using the logo to identify this project**, unaltered and at a legible size,
  in a list of tools, a slide, or an integration directory.

The single test running through all of these: **is it truthful, and would a
reasonable reader understand you are referring to Patchwork OS rather than
claiming to be it?**

## 3. Uses that need permission

Ask first. These are the cases where a reader could be misled about origin or
endorsement.

- **Naming a fork or derivative product with the Marks.** If you fork and
  distribute, give it your own name. "Patchwork OS Plus", "Patchwork OS
  Enterprise", "PatchworkOS.io" and similar are not permitted without written
  permission. You may of course state that your product *is a fork of* Patchwork
  OS — that is §2.
- **Implying endorsement, partnership, certification or affiliation** that does
  not exist. "Official", "certified", "endorsed by", "in partnership with",
  "powered by the Patchwork team".
- **Using the Marks in your company name, product name, domain name, or app
  store listing title.**
- **Modifying the logo**, or using it as your own product's mark.
- **Merchandise** bearing the Marks.
- **Offering a hosted service under the Marks.** Running Patchwork OS as a
  service is permitted by the MIT licence and needs no permission; calling that
  service "Patchwork OS" or "Patchwork Cloud" does need permission, because a
  customer would reasonably read it as operated by this project.

If you have modified the software and are distributing it, the safe pattern is:
**your name, prominently; ours, descriptively.** "Acme Agent Runtime — a fork of
Patchwork OS" is fine. "Acme's Patchwork OS" is not.

## 4. Requesting permission

Open an issue on
[github.com/Oolab-labs/patchwork-os](https://github.com/Oolab-labs/patchwork-os/issues)
describing the intended use, or email legal@oolab.dev.

Include what you want to call the thing, what it is, and how it relates to this
project. Most requests in §3 that are honest about the relationship are easy to
resolve; the ones that are difficult are the ones where the answer to "would a
user think this is the official product?" is yes.
